My love,

There are times when I try to imagine what our future might look like, and somehow, the things I see aren't really the grand things people usually dream about when they talk about forever. I don't immediately picture some enormous house, some perfect life, or some extraordinary adventure where everything falls perfectly into place.

I picture you.
I picture us.
And somehow, that feels so much more beautiful to me.

I imagine mornings where neither of us really wants to leave the warmth of the bed. One of us would already be awake, probably pretending to be asleep just to keep the other there a little longer, until eventually one of us pulls the other back down and quietly says, "Five more minutes." Then five minutes becomes ten, ten becomes twenty, and suddenly we're both laughing because we know perfectly well we're supposed to be doing something else.

I want a life where that kind of thing is ordinary.
Where loving you isn't something I have to wait for special occasions to do.
Where I can wake up and you're simply there.
Where the person I love most in this world is also the person I get to annoy before either of us has even properly started the day.

I want to bake with you too. And knowing us, I'm sure we'd somehow turn something as simple as baking into complete nonsense. We'd probably be arguing over who gets to taste the frosting, pretending not to notice the flour on each other's faces, and eventually one of us would smear icing onto the other's cheek and immediately run away laughing.

And I don't just want the picture-perfect parts of that memory.
I want the messy kitchen.
The failed recipes.
The burnt things we swear we can still eat.
The dishes we don't want to wash afterward.
The laughter that makes us forget what we were even trying to make in the first place.

Because that's what I mean when I say I want a life with you. I don't only want the beautiful moments that would look good written down. I want the moments that nobody else would think are worth remembering.

I want the ordinary.
I want the mundane.
I want the wonderfully uneventful parts of life that somehow become precious simply because you're there.

I want to lie beside you on the bed, or sink into the sofa with you, and talk until we realize we've been talking for hours. I want to hear about the things you're hoping for, the things you're afraid of, the places you want to see, the person you want to become. I want to tell you about my own dreams too, even the ones I haven't figured out yet.

I want us to talk about our future not because we already know exactly what it will be, but because I want to discover it with you.

I want to know what kind of home you'd want.
What kind of mornings you'd like.
Where you'd want us to go.
What we'd save up for.
What we'd laugh about when we're older.
What silly traditions we'd accidentally create without even realizing it.
I want to know all of it.

And maybe one day we'll be standing in the bathroom, doing absolutely nothing important, and somehow we'll start splashing water at each other. You'll get annoyed because I ruined your hair, I'll laugh because you ruined mine too, and eventually we'll just stand there looking at each other like two complete idiots.

I want that too.

I want to yell sweet things at you from the top of the stairs just because I know you'll hear me.
I want to call your name from another room.
I want to hear you answer.
I want to say something completely ridiculous and hear you laugh from somewhere in the house.

I want the kind of love where your presence becomes woven into the background of my life so naturally that even the smallest things begin to carry the warmth of you.

Because I think that's what I mean when I say I want more.

I don't mean that what we have isn't enough.

I mean that somehow, even when I am already given so much of you, my heart still finds itself wanting another day, another conversation, another morning, another memory.

I'm greedy when it comes to you.
But it's a strange kind of greed.
I don't need extravagant things.
I don't need every day to be some grand romantic occasion.

I would be perfectly happy doing the most boring things in the world if I got to do them beside you.

I'd go grocery shopping with you.
I'd wait while you take forever to choose something.
I'd sit beside you while you're busy doing something completely unrelated to me.
I'd accompany you on errands that neither of us particularly wants to do.
I'd even love those quiet moments where we're both doing our own things, but every once in a while we look at each other just to remember that we're there.

Because with you, even boredom sounds like a future I could be happy living in.

And I know I'm going to be the same way even if distance eventually becomes part of our story.

I'd still want to give you things.
I'd still see something and think of you.
I'd still want to surprise you with something small just because I happened to see it and thought, "She'd like this."
I'd still want to send you pieces of my day.
I still want to hear about yours.

Distance could change the way we reach each other, but I don't think it could change how much I want to reach for you.

That's one of the reasons "country" by Lizzy McAlpine makes me think of you.

There's something about that song that makes me think about distance in a way that doesn't feel like an ending. It makes me think about how there can be miles between two people, how there can be waiting, how there can be time that has to pass before you're finally together again, and yet none of that makes the person feel any less worth reaching.

And when I think about you, that's how it feels.

Even if I haven't moved away yet hehe, I still wanted to tell you this because the thought has been sitting with me.

If there ever comes a time when there are cities, countries, or long stretches of time between us, I don't want you to think of that distance as something that makes loving you less worthwhile.

If anything, I think I'd just become more aware of how precious every moment with you is.
Because if I had to cross an ocean just to spend one evening beside you, I'd still want that evening.
If I had to wait months for another ordinary morning with you, I'd still wait for it.
Not because waiting is beautiful by itself, but because you're on the other side of it.

You make the waiting worth it.
You make the distance worth crossing.
You make the time worth spending.

And I think that is one of the most beautiful things about loving someone.
It's not that the world suddenly becomes easy.
It's that one person becomes worth all the difficult parts.

I think about how, back then, I could still tell myself, "Maybe wala lang 'to."
Maybe it was nothing.
Maybe it was just a phase.
Maybe I was simply enjoying having someone around.

But look at me now.
